Milepost

A mileage log for people who deduct their driving. Questions and answers below; anything else, email williamscott701@gmail.com and you'll get a reply from the person who wrote the app.

Common questions

Why does it need location set to "Always"?
Because the whole point is recording drives you didn't remember to start. "While Using the App" only records while Milepost is open on screen, which is almost never while you're driving. If you'd rather not grant it, the manual timer and hand-entered trips work fine.
Will it drain my battery?
Milepost idles on the low-power location signal iOS already computes from cell and wifi transitions, plus the motion coprocessor's activity stream. Full GPS only runs once a drive is actually under way, and a confident "walking" reading switches it back off.
Where does my data go?
Nowhere. There's no account and no server. Your trips and routes live in the app's own storage on your phone.
It missed a drive. What now?
Add it by hand with the + button on the dashboard. If it happens often, open the Glovebox (the car icon, top left) and check that location access is "Always" and that "Record drives automatically" is on.
Why did it call my drive to work personal?
Travel between home and a regular workplace is commuting, and commuting isn't deductible. Milepost applies that rule deliberately, even if you've classified the route differently before. You can still override any single trip.
The distance looks slightly low.
It's meant to. Milepost discards GPS readings it can't trust and rounds down, because claiming fewer miles than you drove is a much smaller problem than claiming more.
What's in the free version?
30 trips a month, classification, your named places and vehicles, and your running deduction total. Full Tank adds every trip, the PDF and CSV report, odometer reconciliation and the pre-filing checks. Milepost keeps recording past the free limit either way — a trip that wasn't recorded can't be recovered later.
I'm not in the United States. Does this work?
Yes. Milepost ships the IRS standard mileage rate for the US and HMRC's approved mileage allowance payments for the UK, including the 45p/25p bands and the 6 April tax year. Anywhere else, pick "Custom rate" in the Glovebox and enter the figure your tax office publishes, along with your currency and whether you count in miles or kilometres. Milepost won't guess a rate for a country it can't verify — that would be worse than asking you.
Why doesn't the UK version have a medical or charity rate?
Because HMRC doesn't publish one. Approved mileage allowance payments cover business travel. Rather than borrow a US figure that doesn't apply, Milepost leaves those blank and says so in the report.
The rate for this year says "unconfirmed".
The IRS publishes the standard mileage rate late in the preceding year, and sometimes changes it mid-year (it did on 1 July 2022 and again on 1 July 2026). Until a rate is confirmed, Milepost carries the latest one forward and marks it rather than guessing. You can enter the published figure yourself in the Glovebox › Mileage rates.
What widgets are there?
Nine, in every size iOS offers: your deduction total, the review queue, this week, this month, a fortnight's activity chart, the split by purpose, your business-use percentage, whether a drive is being recorded, and how much of the free allowance is left. They work on the Home Screen, the Lock Screen and in StandBy, and on iPad.
How do I cancel?
iOS Settings › your name › Subscriptions › Milepost. Cancel at least a day before it renews.

Not tax advice

Milepost keeps a record of your driving and applies the published mileage rate for your region and the dates you travelled. It doesn't know your circumstances and it isn't a substitute for a tax professional.
